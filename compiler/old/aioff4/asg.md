## 1 Assignment Overview

The Travelling Salesman Problem (TSP) is one of the most well-known optimization problems in computer science. A salesman must visit a number of cities exactly once and finally return to the starting city. The objective is to find a tour with the minimum possible total travelling cost or distance.

For this assignment, you will solve the same TSP instance using two different approaches:

- 1. Greedy Technique — to quickly construct a feasible solution.

- 2. Simulated Annealing — a metaheuristic optimization technique designed to im- prove solutions and escape local optima.

You will implement, test, compare, and analyze both techniques.

The purpose of this assignment is not only to obtain a short tour, but also to under- stand the differences between a deterministic greedy heuristic and a probabilistic optimization algorithm.

## 6 Part A: Greedy Technique

## 6.1 Required Algorithm

Implement a greedy heuristic based on the Nearest Neighbor Technique.

Starting from City 0:

- 1. Mark the starting city as visited.

- 2. Among all unvisited cities, select the city with the minimum travelling cost from the current city.

- 3. Move to the selected city.

- 4. Mark it as visited.

- 5. Repeat until all cities have been visited.

- 6. Return to the starting city.

- 7. Calculate the total cost of the complete tour.

## 6.3 Important Observation

The greedy choice produces a good next move based only on current information. How- ever, the cheapest immediate move may lead to an expensive route later.

Therefore:

```
A greedy solution is not guaranteed to be globally optimal.
```

## 7 Part B: Simulated Annealing

## 7.1 Background

Simulated Annealing is inspired by the physical process of annealing in metallurgy.

In physical annealing:

- 1. A material is heated to a high temperature.

- 2. Atoms move relatively freely.

- 3. The material is gradually cooled.

- 4. The atoms eventually settle into a low-energy state.

In optimization:

- A solution corresponds to a physical state.

- The objective function value corresponds to energy.

- A better solution has lower energy.

- The temperature controls the probability of accepting worse solutions.

The important advantage of Simulated Annealing is that it may occasionally accept a worse solution. This allows the algorithm to escape from a local optimum.
